## 1. Identity model

| Entity | What it is | Key in the register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DOCUMENT_COPY** | One stored byte file. Different bytes mean different copies, even when the content is the same. | `sha256` |
| **JUDGMENT** | One decision: a judgment or order pronounced by a court on a date, by a bench. A common judgment that disposes of 40 connected petitions is **one** judgment. A split verdict in two opinions is one judgment. | `judgment_key` (`JK-` plus the first 16 hex digits of the lowest sha256 among its copies). A judgment held *inside* another file gets `JK-<file sha16>#p<pages>`, listed in `additional_judgments_in_file`. |
| **PROCEEDING** | A case disposed of by the judgment (writ petition, appeal, application). One judgment can dispose of many proceedings. One proceeding can have several judgments over time. A one-page pointer order ("For orders, see <lead case>") belongs to its own proceeding, but its decision is the lead judgment. | `proceeding_keys_meta` (court code and crawler case number, complete for the metadata); `proceeding_keys_cause_title_partial` (every case number in the cause title up to CORAM/JUDGMENT/ORDER, at most 3 pages, including bare list continuations; automated and **possibly partial**) |
| **VERSION** | A corrected, amended or re-issued rendition of the *same* judgment whose wording differs (a corrigendum or a "CORRECTED" re-issue). Versions stay one judgment. | `version_role` |
| **SOURCE_RENDITION** | The kind of copy. `OFFICIAL_COURT_PDF` is a PDF generated by the court's system; here it is obtained through the open-data mirror, whose host is recorded in the metadata `source_url`. `OFFICIAL_SYSTEM_TEXT` is a court-issued text or HTML rendering; none of the 3,959 copies is one, and `data/text` is our own extraction, not official. `REPORTER_COPY_SCR_MIRROR` is a Supreme Court Reports page range. `SECONDARY_COPY` is a third-party mirror. | `rendition_class` |

**Merge rule.** Similarity alone never merges files. A judgment entity is a connected set of files linked by one of the following kinds of evidence:
1. **Identical extracted text**, confirmed at byte level or render level.
2. **Same letters and same numbers**, with the same court, decision date and page count. Before comparing, the tool removes clock times (per-download signing stamps) and maps the quote and dash glyphs that one generator extracts as digits (’ as 9, “ as <, ” as =, ‘ as 8, dashes as 3/4).
3. **An incomplete copy that matches on every page readable in both copies**, at letter similarity of at least 0.95. The last page of a truncated copy is compared on its common prefix. There must be at least two common text pages, or an equal case-number set on p.1. A first-page match alone is not enough.
4. **A scanned copy of a common judgment.** It must share all of the following with another copy, and the content match must be confirmed:
   - the same lead case, found in a running footer ("<lead> & connected … Page k of N"), a pointer line ("For orders, see <lead>") or the file's own cause title;
   - the same court;
   - the same decision date (metadata or source-URL date);
   - a page-content match: text shingles on at least 60% of text pages both ways, or page thumbnails with a mean correlation of at least 0.6 over at least 3 pages.

   When a file shows the lead case only in a footer, the text match must reach 0.8 both ways and images do not count. This rule keeps apart W.P.(C) 18383/2025 and 18333/2025: one footer carries a clerk's wrong case number, and the two template orders share 60% of their pages.
5. **A one-page pointer order** ("For orders, see <lead case>"). It is linked to its lead judgment when that judgment is in the corpus with the same court, the lead case number and the same printed or recorded date.

Empty or near-empty text never counts as identical content.
