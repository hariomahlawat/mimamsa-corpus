## Three coverage measures (kept separate)

1. **Automated processing coverage:** 3936 of 3959 source files (99.4%) were opened and processed by the extractor (case numbers, dates, citations, statutes, topics, outcome phrase, candidate propositions, relevance) from copies whose document content is structurally present. Of these, 22 have an earlier end-of-file marker followed by a cut-off trailing update (typically a digital-signature revision): the base revision, and so the text, is present, but the stored file is not byte-complete (`source_completeness` in the copy register). A further 12 were processed from copies with no end-of-file marker at all (stored copy truncated; content may be missing). Their extraction may be partial; they are counted as unresolved, marked `STORED_COPY_TRUNCATED_CONTENT_MAY_BE_MISSING` in every research output, and listed in the continuation manifest for a complete copy.
2. **Source-document verification coverage:** 3955 of 3959 files were re-hashed equal to the HF-declared SHA-256. The bytes came from the GitHub `raw-data` mirror, or for curated binaries from an identical working copy. **HF-hosted bytes themselves: NOT_CHECKED** (no download access; HF revision not pinned).
3. **Independent AI document-review coverage** (not legal review): 30 of 3,959 distinct judgments (0.76%) received independent document-level review by two Fable 5.2 reviewers (claude-fable-5-2), 15 each, against the actual PDF after SHA-256 verification. The sample is the risk-ranked top of five strata (Supreme Court military law, court-martial and AFT jurisdiction, disability pension, termination and natural justice, conflicting or differing data), drawn deterministically from the queue at df832ee1; 28 of the 30 are Supreme Court judgments. Long judgments were read in part (pages listed per row). These are AI reviewer readings, not legal advice and not JAG review. The remaining 3,929 judgments have automated extraction only.

## Measured reliability (independent review of a 30-judgment high-risk sample; details in `review/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`)

- **Outcome candidates were wrong in 15 of 30 and ambiguous in 2.** They are unusable without review for multi-appeal or mixed disposals.
- **Decision-date candidates are unreliable for the Supreme Court layout:** they pick up dates of cited cases or impugned orders. `DIFFERS` on SC records is an extractor signal, not a metadata error.
- **Candidate propositions** are about half the court's own reasoning; the rest are quotations, headnotes or recitals, two of them misleading. They are leads only.
- **Metadata gap:** 368 Supreme Court records have no case number in the crawler metadata, although the documents print one.
- Crawler metadata dates were right in 29 of 30, and file identity in 30 of 30.

## Measured reliability on ordinary judgments (workstream review of 20 randomly drawn, non-sampled judgments; `review/WS_REVIEW_SAMPLE20_3257ebb9.csv`)

- **Outcome candidate:** right in 12, wrong in 3, missed in 4, not applicable in 1. All three wrong ones took 'pending applications stand disposed of' as the result of a petition that was dismissed or allowed.
- **Crawler metadata was more reliable than the extractor on every field measured.** Where crawler outcome and text candidate disagreed in the sample, the crawler was right 3 times out of 4.
- Corpus-wide, crawler outcome and text candidate disagree in **549 of 1775** records that have both. At least some pairs differ only in vocabulary (the extractor emits `remanded`, `set_aside_quashed` and `transferred`, which the crawler never uses). Each such record carries a contradiction note in `HF_JUDGMENT_METADATA_AUDIT.csv` and is raised in the verification queue. **Prefer the crawler outcome; use the text candidate only as a review signal.**
- Case-number confirmation is limited by the pattern list (for example SWP, OWP, LPASW and CONT.CAS(C) numbers are not recognised). Such misses are NOT_FOUND or NOT_CONFIRMED, never contradictions.

## Limits of the excluded-log title re-screen

- The crawler kept no files for the 120,185 excluded judgments, so only titles could be re-screened. The rule covers service, ministry and defence terms, the abbreviation M.O.D. and rank prefixes. Titles that abbreviate a ministry differently, or that concern defence land (Defence Estates) rather than service, may be over- or under-flagged. A flag is a lead for re-acquisition, which needs approval; it is not a relevance finding.

## Limits

- Automated extraction can be wrong: dates, case numbers, outcome phrases and propositions are candidates for human checking (see `HF_JUDGMENT_VERIFICATION_QUEUE.csv`).
- Relevance is assigned by fixed term rules. The excluded log was filtered by the crawler for military keywords, and its files were not retained. Re-screening those judgments for general-law relevance needs the files, and acquiring them is not authorised here. Titles were re-screened instead.
- No judgment has been legally verified. Holdings are not cleared for use.
