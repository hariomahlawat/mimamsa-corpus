## State (NCT of Delhi) v Navjot Sandhu

**Curated ID:** `DCLR-SC-2005-L01`  
**Decision:** 4 August 2005  
**Documentary source:** `legal_knowledge/judgment_originals/sci/historical/v3.3/navjot_sandhu_2005.pdf`  
**Source tier:** `SUPREME_COURT_JUDIS_PRIMARY`  
**Review locators:** 1–2 (identity/connected appeals); 13–15 (Section 27 discussion); later electronic evidence portions require pinpoint completion

### Material facts / procedural posture
Connected appeals arose from the December 2001 Parliament attack prosecution. The decision addressed confessions, discovery, electronic material and multiple accused; it contains numerous fact-specific and statutory questions.

### Questions before the Court
Whether particular confessional/discovery material and electronic records could be admitted or relied upon under the law and record before the Court.

### Historical statutory framework
Indian Evidence Act 1872 sections 27, 63, 65 and 65B; POTA as in force in 2001–05; Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 requires separate current-law treatment

### Narrow holding candidate (not yet JAG validated)
The original decision discussed both Section 27 discovery and secondary electronic evidence. These are separate propositions. Its approach permitting alternative secondary electronic evidence proof in the absence of a Section 65B certificate was later rejected by Anvar P.V. and discussed by Arjun Panditrao and Chandrabhan Sudam Sanap (2025). Ravishankar Tandon (2024) separately discusses the Section 27 reasoning. Do not label the entire 2005 judgment overruled.

### Final disposition and relief
A connected multi-appellant criminal decision; individual convictions and relief are distinct. No single undifferentiated disposition should be assigned to every person named in the cause title.

### Temporal and factual limits
The former Evidence Act and POTA-era facts are historically situated. Current treatment under BSA 2023 and the special military evidence regime must be checked independently.

### Subsequent precedent treatment
2024 INSC 299 discussed Section 27; 2025 INSC 116 discusses negative treatment concerning electronic evidence under former Section 65B. Verify actual later SC opinions and operative dates.
