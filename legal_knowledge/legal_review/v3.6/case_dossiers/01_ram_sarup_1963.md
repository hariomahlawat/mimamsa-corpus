## Ram Sarup v Union of India

**Curated ID:** `DCLR-SC-1964-A11`  
**Decision:** 12 December 1963  
**Documentary source:** `legal_knowledge/judgment_originals/sci/historical/v3.3/ram_sarup_1963.pdf`  
**Source tier:** `SUPREME_COURT_JUDIS_PRIMARY`  
**Review locators:** 1–2 (headnote); 7–10 (judicial reasoning and outcome)

### Material facts / procedural posture
A sepoy challenged the outcome of a General Court-Martial involving offences under Army Act section 69 read with IPC section 302. The controversy included forum allocation between ordinary criminal courts and court-martial, confirmation and constitutional challenges.

### Questions before the Court
Whether the discretion under Army Act section 125 to select the forum for trial was unguided or unconstitutional, and how Article 33 affects statutory provisions restricting military personnel's fundamental rights.

### Historical statutory framework
Constitution Article 33; Army Act 1950 sections 69, 125, 126 and 164; historic Code of Criminal Procedure section 549

### Narrow holding candidate (not yet JAG validated)
The Court rejected the challenge to section 125 and held the choice of trial forum was controlled by the statutory scheme and inter-authority procedures, not an arbitrary unfettered discretion. The historic Article 33 reasoning addresses Parliament's power to modify rights for armed forces discipline; it is not a general statement that service personnel lack constitutional protections.

### Final disposition and relief
Writ petition dismissed. Original court source indicates the petition was decided in 1963 although later reported in the 1964 SCR.

### Temporal and factual limits
The older CrPC section 549 framework and the 1963 Army Act context require a current-law crosswalk. Do not confuse Article 33 modification with an unrestricted waiver of procedural fairness.

### Subsequent precedent treatment
Review the Court's subsequent decisions on forum allocation, Army Act sections 125–126 and modern criminal procedure; do not mechanically apply 1963 procedural steps to 2026.
