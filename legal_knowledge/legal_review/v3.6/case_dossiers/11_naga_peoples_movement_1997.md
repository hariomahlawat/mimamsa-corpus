## Naga People's Movement of Human Rights v Union of India

**Curated ID:** `DCLR-SC-1997-L11`  
**Decision:** 27 November 1997  
**Documentary source:** `data/raw/sci/1997/00a6512e374c880e113494dfc51e085bb91d0fc134a0aa2cb09daa739e278531.pdf`  
**Source tier:** `PUBLISHED_SCR_COPY_SHA_VERIFIED_JUDIS_BYTE_PARITY_UNKNOWN`  
**Review locators:** 1–2 (SCR headnote); verify operative directions in later opinion pages 40–55

### Material facts / procedural posture
Petitioners challenged legislative competence, constitutionality and certain powers under the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act in affected northeastern areas.

### Questions before the Court
Whether the Act and disturbed-area declarations could be constitutionally sustained and which restraints governed exercise of the powers.

### Historical statutory framework
AFSPA 1958 sections 3–6; constitutional distribution of legislative competence and safeguards

### Narrow holding candidate (not yet JAG validated)
The 1997 decision sustained the basic constitutional framework of AFSPA while addressing restrictions, safeguards and periodic review of disturbed-area declarations. It must not be represented as unlimited immunity for unlawful force.

### Final disposition and relief
Constitutional challenge substantially rejected subject to the Court's interpretation and safeguards; review each operative order rather than relying on the SCR headnote alone.

### Temporal and factual limits
The exact geographic notification in force on a particular date, central/state notification distinctions, proportionality, lawful force and later EEVFAM decisions are indispensable before any operational advice.

### Subsequent precedent treatment
Review EEVFAM 2016 and 2017 Supreme Court judgments and current Gazette territorial notifications. The 1997 judgment cannot by itself establish 2026 AFSPA coverage in any named location.
