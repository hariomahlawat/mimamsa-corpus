It has been submitted by the learned counsel for the petitioner that
the petitioner was enrolled in the Army on 05.05.2015. At the time of
his enrollment, a verification of his character was made and nothing
adverse was found against him. Subsequently, a complaint was
submitted, pursuant to which a re-verification was conducted. The
District Magistrate, Etawah, by his letter dated 09.11.2016, intimated
that the petitioner was an accused in a criminal case under Sections
498A, 323, 504, and 506 of the I.P.C., read with Section 3/4 of the
Dowry Prohibition Act. On the basis of such information, a show
cause notice was issued to the petitioner and he was later dismissed
from service.

The petitioner filed a representation against such dismissal, which
was also rejected. Aggrieved, the petitioner filed Original Application
No. 391 of 2020 before the Armed Forces Tribunal, Regional Bench,
Lucknow, praying for quashing of the discharge order dated
15.12.2016 and for his reinstatement in service.

It has further been submitted by the learned counsel for the petitioner
that the petitioner was only 15 years of age at the time when the
F.I.R. was lodged against him on 16.04.2013 under Sections 498A,
323, 504, and 506 of the I.P.C., read with Section 3/4 of the Dowry
Prohibition Act, by his sister-in-law, who had a matrimonial dispute
with his brother. The petitioner was never informed by the
Investigating Officer that he was required in the said criminal case,
which had been lodged at a police station different from the police
station where the petitioner resided. He was never informed of any
summons issued to him as an accused by any Court or Magistrate on
the basis of the charge-sheet submitted by the Investigating Officer.
Therefore, at the time of signing the application form, in response to
the query made at point no.7(1) of the verification roll, the petitioner
had replied "No." The said answer was treated by the respondents as
a deliberate misrepresentation amounting to fraud, on the basis of
which his services were terminated as aforesaid.

There is no finding recorded regarding whether the petitioner had any
knowledge of the F.I.R. lodged against him by his sister-in-law in April
2013. Moreover, the petitioner was a student of Class 9 and only 15
years old at the time and was, therefore, a juvenile.

The Tribunal has also not recorded any finding as to whether the
petitioner had deliberately misrepresented or played fraud upon the
competent authority. Instead, it merely relied upon the facts stated by
the respondents and upon case law relating to fraud, without first
recording a finding as to whether any fraud was actually committed
by the petitioner.

We have gone through the judgment and order impugned and find
that the questions framed by the Tribunal have been mentioned in
paragraph 6 of the impugned judgment; however, the Tribunal has
not framed any question as to whether the applicant was aware of the
F.I.R. lodged against him by his sister-in-law in April 2013, when he
was only 15 years of age and whether, despite such knowledge, he
had submitted incorrect information in the verification roll.

We, therefore, set aside the judgment and order impugned and
remand the matter to the Armed Forces Tribunal to frame an issue as
to whether the applicant had any knowledge of his being an accused
in the F.I.R. lodged against him on 16.04.2013, then record a finding
thereon and pass an appropriate order in the light of the relevant
case laws.

Accordingly, the writ petition is disposed of.
