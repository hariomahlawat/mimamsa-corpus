4.
Respondent No.1 is stated to have suffered disability during his

service and was discharged w.e.f. 01.02.1983. His disability had been assessed at

11% to 14% for life by the RSMB on 19.09.2002. He preferred O.A. No.1303 of

2020 seeking inter alia grant of disability pension.

5.
The Tribunal vide the impugned order had disposed of the

application with the direction to the petitioners to process the applicant’s claim

for disability pension in terms of the judgments of the Supreme Court in the case

of Union of India and others Vs. Ram Avtar, 2014 SCC Online SC 1761.

6.
We do not find any merit in the argument of learned counsel for the

petitioners that the application of respondent No.1 suffers from delay and laches,

for the reason that pension is a recurring cause of action, which has been

reiterated by the Supreme Court in the case of M.L. Patil (dead) Through LRs

v. State of Goa and another, 2022 SCC Online 685. So far as the second

argument of learned counsel for the petitioners is concerned that the disability of

respondent No.1 had been assessed by the Board as less than 20% which would

make him ineligible for disability pension, the same has been rejected by the

Supreme Court in the case of Sukhvinder Singh Vs. Union of India (supra).

7.
Furthermore, the benefit of rounding off the disability to the extent

of 50% has been affirmed in the judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of

Union of India and others Vs. Ram Avtar (supra). It is also not in dispute that

the Supreme Court in its judgment passed in the case of Bijender Singh Vs.

U.O.I. and Ors., 2025 SCC Online SC 895 has affirmed the judgments passed

in cases of Union of India v. Ram Avtar (supra) and Sukhvinder Singh Vs.

Union of India, (supra) and had reiterated that wherever a member of the

Armed Forces is invalided out of service, it perforce has to be assumed that his

8.
It is also apt to notice that this petition has been preferred after a
