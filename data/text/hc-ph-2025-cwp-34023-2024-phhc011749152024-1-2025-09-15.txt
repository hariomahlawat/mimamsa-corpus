1.
In the present petition, the challenge is to the impugned order

dated 21.02.2019 (Annexure P-1)  passed by respondent No.2-Armed Forces

Tribunal, Regional Bench, Chandigarh (for short, ‘Tribunal’) by which,

respondent No.1 has been allowed the benefit of disability pension by

rounding off the disability element from 20% to 50% for life on the ground

that the same is perverse.

2.
Learned counsel for the petitioners places reliance upon the

report of medical report of respondent No. 1 to hold that though the

disability of “DM Type-II (E-11)”, which has been assessed @ 20% has

been found in respondent No.1, but the said disability has been held to be

not attributable but was only held to be aggravated by the Military service.

Hence, the grant of benefit of disability pension by rounding off the

6.
Learned counsel for the petitioners has not been able to dispute

the fact that under the rules governing the grant of the disability pension,

either the disability suffered by the officer concerned while in service should

be attributed to the military service or should have been aggravated by the

military service.

7.
In the present case, it is conceded before this Court that the

disability on the basis of which the disability pension is being sought by the

respondent No.1 has been held to be aggravated by the military service as

per the finding recorded by the Medical Board therefore, learned counsel for

the petitioners has not been able to dispute the entitlement of the disability

pension once the disability suffered is aggravated by the military service.

Apart from this, it is to be seen whether the said disability can be attributed

to the military service or not.

8.
It is conceded fact that at the time when respondent No.1 was

discharged from service on 30.04.2016, he had already rendered 33 years of

service with the petitioners-Union of India.  It is also a conceded fact that at

the time when respondent No. 1 joined the armed forces i.e. on 08.11.1983

he was medically examined and was found not to be suffering from any such

disease. This had been made the basis by the Tribunal while granting benefit

to the respondent No.1 by placing reliance upon Dharamvir Singh (supra).

It should be noted that as per Dharamvir Singh’s case (supra),

personnel who has suffered disability that same is attributable to and

aggravated by military service, and the onus of proof is upon the employer

‘rounding off of disability pension’ the same issue has been settled by the

Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in Union of India and others vs. Ram

12.
Further, in a recent judgment in Civil Appeal No.11311 of 2025

titled as Union of India and others vs. Reet MP Singh and another,

decided on 01.09.2025, the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India by placing

reliance upon  Union of India and others vs. Ram Avtar, 2014 SCC Online

SC 1761 as well as Bijender Singh vs. Union of India and others, 2025

SCC Online SC 895, has again reiterated that the benefit of rounding off the

disability element cannot be denied.

13.
Further, with regard to the argument of the learned counsel for

the petitioner that the judgment passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court of

India in Narsingh’s case (supra)  has not been considered by the Tribunal

while passing the order impugned, it may be noticed that the judgment

passed  in Narsingh’s case (supra)  only states that the findings of the

medical board  should not be overturned  in a routine manner. The bare

perusal of the records as place don page No.57 of the petition shows that the

disability DM Type-II (E-II) was aggravated by military service. Nothing

evident has been brought on record by the petitioners as to why the disease

suffered by the respondent cannot be attributed to the military service or

aggravated by  the military service especially when respondent does not

suffer the same at the time of recruitment in Army, hence, judgment in

Narsingh’s case (supra)   cannot be brought in operation in the present case.

14.
Keeping in view the facts and circumstance of the present case

as well as the settled principle of law settled in Dharamvir Singh’s case
