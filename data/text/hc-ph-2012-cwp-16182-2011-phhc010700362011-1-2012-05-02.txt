T.P.S. MANN, J.

The petitioner has filed the present writ petition under

Articles 226/227 of the Constitution of India for challenging the order

dated 22.7.2010 passed by the Chandigarh Regional Bench of the

Armed Forces Tribunal, Chandigarh (hereinafter referred to as ‘the

Tribunal’) whereby T.A. No.34 of 2009 filed by him challenging the

rejection of his prayer for grant of disability pension for want of

medical record had been dismissed.

Undisputedly, the petitioner was enrolled in the Army in

the year 1962 and was discharged on 14.11.1970.  In the year

1977, his claim for disability pension was declined by the Principal

Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension), Allahabad on the ground

that the so-called disability was neither attributable to nor

aggravated by military service.  More than 30 years thereafter, the

petitioner filed the writ petition in this Court in the year 2008 which

was, later on, transferred to the Tribunal and, accordingly,

numbered as T.A. No.34 of 2009. In order to substantiate that the

disability was attributable to or aggravated by military service, the

petitioner did not place any material on the record.  Under these

circumstances, the Tribunal held that no reliance could be placed

on mere ipse dixit of the petitioner that he had fallen sick and was

sent to the Army Hospital on account of extreme climatic conditions

in the Army, where he was on active duty or that he was, later on,

declared a case of Narwa.

Having heard the learned counsel for the petitioner, we

find that the petitioner had not been able to establish his claim for

the grant of disability pension that his disability was either

attributable to or aggravated by the military service.  Moreover, the

petitioner remained silent for a period of more than 30 years and it

was only in the year 2008 that he filed the writ petition in this Court

for his claim for disability pension having been refused by the

Principal Controller of Defence Accounts (Pension), Allahabad in

the year 1977.

In view of the above, no case is made out for showing

any indulgence to the petitioner.

The petition is, accordingly, dismissed.
