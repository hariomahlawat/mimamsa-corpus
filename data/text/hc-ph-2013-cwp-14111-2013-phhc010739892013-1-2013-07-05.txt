S.S. SARON, J.

This writ petition has been filed by the petitioner seeking

quashing of the order dated  01.08.2012 (Annexure P-9) passed by

the Armed Forces Tribunal Chandigarh Regional Bench at

Chandigarh (Tribunal, for short) whereby the original application filed

by the petitioner for grant of disability pension has been rejected as

also for quashing the order dated 28.04.2011 (Annexure P-6)

whereby the petitioner has been held disentitled to disability pension

by the competent authority.  A further prayer has been made for

directing the respondent to grant disability pension to the petitioner

w.e.f. 01.09.2010 when the petitioner was invalided from military

service due to the disability which according to the petitioner had

arisen during his military service.

The petitioner was enrolled in the Indian Army in the Signal

Corps on 05.03.2009.  At the time of his enrolment, it is submitted

[[page 2]]
CWP No.14111 of 2013

that he was not suffering from any kind of disability and was in

shape-I.  He was sent to South India for military training.  During

training, due to stress and strain of the service, he suffered disease

of 'schizophrenia'.  He was invalided  from military service with 20%

disability.  It was, however, submitted by the authorities that the

disability of the petitioner was neither attributable to nor aggravated

as a result of military service.  The petitioner is now left at home

without any financial assistance.  It is submitted that during training a

person has to go through various difficult tasks; besides, face difficult

situations.  Due to the stress and strain of the tasks that the

petitioner performed during his training sessions in the middle of May

2009, he developed psychosis problem.  He started hearing the

voices of his family members and of strangers discussing about him,

while he was in a class room.  Later on this problem aggravated due

to the stress and strain of his service.

The training centre on 17.06.2010 wrote a letter (Annexure

P-1) to his father asking him to approach the office to discuss the

matter regarding his son who was undergoing training at the

regiment as his actions suggested that he was mentally unstable and

had shown unwillingness to continue service in the Army.  According

to the petitioner, a bare perusal of the said letter (Annexure P-1)

shows that  it had been categorically stated that the petitioner was

mentally unstable.  Thereafter, the training centre on 03.07.2010 sent

another letter (Annexure P-2) to his father stating that the petitioner

had been admitted in the Military Hospital, Panaji on 19.06.2010 and

he has been transferred to the Command Hospital, Southern

Command, Pune on 27.06.2010 along with two guards as he was

under psychiatric case (sic. treatment).  The petitioner was thereafter

discharged from the Command Hospital Southern Command, Pune

on 13.11.2010.  In terms of the Hospital Discharged Slip (Annexure

P-3) the case summary of the petitioner was mentioned and he was

diagnosed as a case of 'schizophrenia'. The petitioner was

recommended to be invalided from military service with in two years

of service.  An invalid medical board was constituted and in terms of

its proceedings (Annexure P-4), the medical board noticed the

disability of the petitioner as Schizophrenia.  After examination and

consultation it was considered prudent to invalid him from service.

According to the petitioner he was fit at the time of his enrolment in

the army and later on he was invalided out in low medical  category

with  Schizophrenia which was assessed to the extent of 20% for life.

After his invalidment from the military service, the petitioner

applied for disability pension.  However, vide impugned order dated

28.04.2011 (Annexure P-6), the competent authority held that he was

not entitled to disability pension in terms of Regulation 179 of the

Pension Regulations for the Army 1961, Part-I ('Regulations'-for

short) since the disability of Schizophrenia as recorded in the release

medical Board proceedings was found to be neither attributable to

nor aggravated by military service.  In case the petitioner was  not

satisfied with the said decision, it was informed that he may prefer an

appeal to the Appellate Committee on First Appeals (ACFA) against

the decision within six months from the date of receipt of the letter.

The petitioner served a legal notice (Annexure P-7) to the

respondent through his counsel on 17.06.2011.  The respondent

authorities, however, did not initiate any proceedings so as to satisfy

the valid claim of the petitioner.  After waiting for  quite a substantial

period the petitioner filed a representation (Annexure P-8) on

21.11.2011.  Thereafter according to the petitioner he had been

pursuing his case.  The petitioner aggrieved against the order dated

28.04.2011 (Annexure P-6) filed an original application before the

Tribunal which has been dismissed vide impugned order dated

01.08.2012 (Annexure P-9) which is assailed by the petitioner.

Learned counsel for the petitioner contends that the

petitioner is entitled for disability pension in terms of Regulation 173

of the Regulations as he suffered Schizophrenia which was on

account of stress and strain of military service and is attributable to

and aggravated by military service.

We have given our thoughtful consideration to the matter.

The petitioner as already noticed, had joined the mililtary service on

05.03.2009.  During service he was diagnosed as a case of

Schizophrenia. The father of the petitioner was informed about his

unstable condition and also his unwillingness to continue in service in

the Army and about his being shifted to Pune for Psychiatric treatment

vide letters dated 17.06.2010 (Annexure P-1) and 27.06.2010

(Annexure P-2).  He was treated at the Command Hospital, Southern

Command, Pune.  In terms of the discharge slip (Annexure P-3), it is

[[page 5]]
CWP No.14111 of 2013
5

recorded as DGAFMS memorandum 171 of 2002 recommends

invalidment in cases of psychosis with onset within two years of

service.  The proceedings of the Medical Board (Annexure P-4)

mention his disability as Schizophrenia (F-20).  The case of the

petitioner is that he is entitled for disability pension in terms of

Regulation 173 which reads as under:-

“Primary conditions for the grant of disability

pension.

173 Unless otherwise specifically provided a

disability pension consisting of service element and

disability element may be granted to an individual who is

invalided out of service on account of a disability which is

attributable to or aggravated by military service in non battle

casualty and is assessed 20 per cent or over.

The question whether a disability is attributable to

or aggravated by militaty service shall be determined under

the rule in Appendix II.

A perusal of the above shows that disability element may be

granted to an individual who is invalided out of service on account of

disability which is attributable to or aggravated by military service  in

non battle casualty and is assessed at 20 per cent or over.  The

question whether a disability is attributable to or aggravated by

military service is to be determined in terms of the rule in Appendix II

of the Regulations.

Learned counsel for the petitioner has contended that a

[[page 6]]
CWP No.14111 of 2013
6

presumption as provided in the rule to Appendix II particularly Rule 5

of Appendix II of the Regulations is liable to be drawn in favour of the

petitioner and he be held entitled to disability pension.  Rule 5 of

Appendix of the Regulations reads as under:-

“5. The approach to the question of entitlement to

casualty pensionary awards and evaluation of disabilities

shall be based on the following presumptions:-

Prior to and during service

(a) member is presumed to have been in sound

physical and mental condition upon entering service except

as to physical disabilities noted or recorded at the time of

entrance.

(b)  In the event of his subsequently being

discharged from service on medical grounds any

deterioration in his health which has taken place is due to

service.”

No doubt there is a presumption in terms of the above Rule

that in the event an individual is discharged from service on medical

grounds, the deterioration in the health that has taken place is due to

service. However, a presumption is mostly rebutable as to the facts

which are to be taken as proved and it is to apply till the contrary is

not proved.  When a presumption is rebutable it only means that the

party on whom the burden lies is taken to be correct but when the

other side produces material to the contrary fairly and reasonably,

the real fact is not to be taken as presumed and the presumption that

[[page 7]]
CWP No.14111 of 2013
7

may be said to be raised, no longer subsists.

In the present case the Tribunal referred to the proceedings

of the Medical Board and to Para 54 of the Guide to Medical Officers,

2008 which reads as under:-

“MENTAL
AND

endogenous

and

This is true for the entire spectrum of psychiatric disorders

(Psychosis and neurosis) including substance abuse

disorders.  The relative contribution of each, of course,

varies from one diagnostic category to another and from

case to case.  The concept of attributability or aggravation

due to the stress and strain of military service can be,

therefore, evaluated independent of the diagnosis and will

be determined by the specific circumstances of each case.

operational area

(iv)
Diving or submarine accidents, lost at sea.

Notwithstanding the disability having arisen in service, there

is nothing to show that anything related to service conditions

contributed to or aggravated the disability.  Here it will be

relevant to refer to Rule 14 (c) of entitlement Rules for

casualty pensionary awards, 1982 which reads as under:-

[[page 10]]

10

14  (c)
If the disease is accepted as having

arisen in service, it must also be established that

the conditions of military service determined or

contributed to the onset of the disease and that the

conditions were due to the circumstances of duty

in military service.”

Besides, it was noticed that the petitioner himself did not

want to continue in service in the Army.  This in fact is evident even

from the initial letter dated 17.06.2010 (Annexure P-1) that was

addressed to the father of the petitioner in which it is mentioned that

the action of the petitioner suggests that he is mentally unstable and

has shown unwillingness to continue service in the Army.  There is

nothing to controvert the said factual position. Therefore, the

presumption that is sought to be raised in terms of rule to Appendix II

of the Regulations would be clearly inapplicable and would stand

dislodged by the evidence produced by the respondents tending to

show that the real fact is not as presumed.

Therefore, there is no merit in the petition and the same is

accordingly dismissed.
