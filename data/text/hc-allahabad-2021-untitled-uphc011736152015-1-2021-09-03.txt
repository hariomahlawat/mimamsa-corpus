Court No. - 49

Case :- APPLICATION U/S 482 No. - 11618 of 2015

Applicant :- Lt.Col.Mukul Chauhan
Opposite Party :- State Of U.P. And 3 Others
Counsel for Applicant :- Shishir Prakash,Shad Khan
Counsel for Opposite Party :- Govt. Advocate

Hon'ble Vivek Agarwal,J.

1. Sri Lt. Col. Mukul Chauhan (in person) and Sri Nagendra
Srivastava, learned AGA for the State.

2. Sri Lt. Col. Mukul Chauhan submits that he has discharged
his counsel Sri Shad Khan and wants to argue this matter on his
own.

3. He is granted liberty to discharge his counsel Sri Shad Khan
and argue this matter personally.

4. Applicant has filed this application under Section 482 Cr.P.C.
seeking invocation of inherent powers under Section 482
Cr.P.C. and to quash the order dated 16.04.2015 passed by
learned Additional Principal Judge Family Court, Meerut in
Case No.158 of 2014, under Section 125 Cr.P.C., Police Station
Daurala, District Meerut and further seeking a direction to the
court below to adjudicate the issue regarding the applicability
of the statute/Act on the opposite parties no.2 to 4.

5. Applicant has placed reliance on a decision of Bombay High
Court in case of Bhagwan Raoji Dale vs. Sushma Alias Nanda
Bhagwan Dale reported in 1 (1999) DMC 168.

6. The only issue raised by him is that a divorced wife is not
entitled to claim maintenance in term of the provisions
contained in Explanation (b) Below Section 125 Cr.P.C.
According to the applicant if a divorce is given by the husband
through the process of law and not unilaterally as is permissible
under the Muslim Personal Law, then in that event a divorced
wife is not entitled to clam maintenance even if she has not
remarried.

7. Sri Lt. Col. Mukul Chauhan submits that their marriage was
performed on 16.12.2000 at Meerut. He filed divorce petition
and obtained divorce decree on 13.02.2012 from Family Court,
Meerut. This was ex-parte divorce decree. He admits that his
wife had filed first appeal against ex-parte decree of divorce
which was registered as First Appeal No.494 of 2013.

[[page 2]]
According to him, this appeal was dismissed on 14.12.2016 for
want of prosecution and according to him no restoration has
been filed till date.

8. Sri Lt. Col. Mukul Chauhan further submits that his son is
now major. He has already moved an application under Section
257 Cr.P.C. for withdrawal from the case filed by his wife- Smt.
Ranjana Chauhan. He further admits that his daughter, namely,
Ms. Indrani Chauhan  is getting maintenance @ Rs.19,000/- per
month in terms of the provisions contained in Section 90 (I) of
the Army Act.

9. Thus, the moot question which is to be decided is that,
whether in the light of the law laid down in case of Bhagwan
Raoji Dale Vs. Sushma Alias Nanda Bhagwan Dale (supra),
whether proceedings before the learned Additional Principal
Judge, Family Court, Meerut in Case No.158 of 2014, under
Section 125 Cr.P.C., are required to be quashed on the ground
that applicant-Lt. Col. Mukul Chauhan had obtained ex parte
divorce from his wife on 13.02.2012.

10. In this regard, judgment of Supreme Court in case of
Rohtash Singh Vs. Smt. Ramendri and others; AIR 2000 SC
952 is important. Dealing with the Explanation given below
Section 125(1)(b) Cr.P.C., it is held that a woman who has been
divorced by her husband on account of a decree passed by the
learned Family Court under the Hindu Marriage Act continues
to enjoy the status of a wife for the limited purpose of claiming
maintenance allowance from ex-husband. Hon'ble Supreme
Court has quoted from its earlier decision in case of Ramesh
Chander Kaushal Vs. Mrs. Veena Kaushal, AIR 1978 SC
1807, where it is held that:-

"9. This provision is a measure of social justice and specially enacted to
protect women and children and falls within the constitutional sweep of
Article 15(3) reinforced by Article 39. We have no doubt that sections of
statutes calling for construction by Courts are not petrified print but
vibrant words with social functions to fulfil. The brooding presence of the
constitutional empathy for the weaker sections like women and children
must inform interpretation if it has to have social relevance. So viewed it
is possible to be selective in picking out that interpretation out of two
alternatives which advances the cause - the cause of the derelicts."

11. Placing reliance on para-9A and para-10 of the Supreme
Court judgment in case of Rohtash Singh Vs. Smt. Ramendri
and others; (supra), which reads as under:-

"9A. Claim for maintenance under the first part of Section 125, Cr.P.C. is
based on the subsistence of marriage while claim for maintenance of a
divorced wife is based on the foundation provided by Explanation (b) to;
Sub-section (1) of Section 125, Cr.P.C. If the divorced wife is unable to

[[page 3]]
maintain herself and if she has not remarried, she will be entitled to
Maintenance Allowance. The Calcutta High Court had an occasion to
consider an identical situation where the husband had obtained divorce on
the ground of desertion by wife but she was held entitled to Maintenance
Allowance as a divorced wife under Section 125, Cr. P.C. and the fact that
she had deserted her husband and on that basis a decree for divorce was
passed against her was not treated as a bar to her claim for maintenance
as a divorced wife. The Allahabad High Court also, in the instant case,
has taken a similar view. We approve these decisions as they represent the
correct legal position.

10. Learned counsel for the petitioner then submitted that once a decree
for divorce was passed against the respondent and marital relations
between the petitioner and the respondent came to an end, the mutual
rights, duties and obligations should also come to an end. He pleaded that
in this situation, the obligation of the petitioner to maintain a woman with
whom all relations came to an end should also be treated to have come to
an end. This plea, as we have already indicated above, cannot be accepted
as a woman has two distinct rights for maintenance. As a wife, she is
entitled to maintenance unless she suffers from any of the disabilities
indicated in Section 125(4). In another capacity, namely, as a divorced
woman, she is again entitled to claim maintenance from the person of
whom she was once the wife. A woman after divorce becomes a destitute.
If she cannot maintain herself or remains unmarried, the man who was,
once, her husband continues to be under a statutory duty and obligation to
provide maintenance to her."

13. This aspect of law as propounded by Hon'ble Supreme
Court by implication overrules the decision of Bombay High
Court in case of Bhagwan Raoji Dale Vs. Sushma Alias Nanda
Bhagwan Dale (supra), therefore, unless and until, applicant is
in a position to demonstrate that his divorced wife is in a
position to maintain herself or that she has re-married, she will
be entitled to maintenance allowance and, therefore, the
Application deserves to be dismissed and is dismissed, as such.
