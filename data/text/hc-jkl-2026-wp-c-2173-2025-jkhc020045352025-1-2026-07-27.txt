1.      Through the medium of instant Writ Petition, petitioner seeks the

following reliefs:-

a)
Issue a writ of mandamus or any other appropriate writ, order

or direction, directing respondent Nos.1 to 3 to initiate a time bound

and impartial inquiry into the recruitment documents of GDR

Parshotam Singh (Army No.271811Y);

b)
Direct the respondents to take disciplinary and legal action

against the said individual in accordance with the provisions of the

Indian Army Act and the BNS, if the allegations are substantiated;

2.
The case projected by the petitioner in the present writ petition is that his

daughter was married to respondent No.4 on 07.11.2021. It is averred that

within one year of the solemnization of the marriage, the petitioner's

daughter committed suicide. Consequently, FIR No.87 dated 03.05.2022

came to be registered against respondent No.4 and his family members,

wherein the investigation has culminated in the filing of a challan before

Sr. No. 30

the competent Court at Samba. It is further pleaded that the petitioner also

instituted a complaint under Section 406 of the Indian Penal Code read

with Section 6 of the Dowry Prohibition Act against respondent No. 4 and

his family members, which is presently pending before the learned Chief

Judicial Magistrate, Samba. The order dated 07.01.2023 passed by the

learned Chief Judicial Magistrate, Samba, was challenged by respondent

No. 4 and the other accused before this Court, and the operation thereof

came to be stayed vide order dated 12.05.2023.

3.
The petitioner has further averred that respondent No. 4, with a deliberate

and mala fide intention, suppressed material facts pertaining to his

identity and credentials, thereby inducing the petitioner and his family to

enter into the matrimonial alliance. It is alleged that, in furtherance of

such misrepresentation, the date of birth of respondent No. 4 has been

reflected as 28.06.2000 in the Marriage Certificate (Annexure-V) issued

by the Registrar of Marriages, Suchetgarh, despite his spouse being

recorded therein as 28 years of age. The petitioner has further relied upon

the certificate (Annexure-IV) issued by the Principal, Martyr Major

Arvind Bazala Government (Boys) Model Higher Secondary School, R.S.

Pura, wherein the date of birth of respondent No. 4, namely Parshotam

Singh, is recorded as 28.02.1991. According to the petitioner, a fabricated

certificate reflecting his date of birth as 28.06.2000 was subsequently

furnished at the time of his recruitment in the Indian Army. It is further

pleaded that the petitioner submitted detailed complaints, duly supported

by documentary material, before respondent No. 3 seeking appropriate

action. However, despite the said representations, no disciplinary,

administrative, or preliminary enquiry has been initiated, thereby

constraining the petitioner to invoke the extraordinary writ jurisdiction of

this Court.

4.
Per contra, learned counsel appearing for the respondents vehemently

opposed the writ petition and contended that the same is not maintainable.

It was submitted that, at the instance of the petitioner, a criminal case

already stands registered against respondent No.4 and his family

members, and the competent Criminal Court is presently seized of the

matter. It was further argued that the present petition has been instituted

only with a view to settle personal scores and constitutes an abuse of the

writ jurisdiction of this Court. Learned counsel, therefore, prayed for

dismissal of the writ petition in limine.

5.
I have heard learned counsel appearing for the parties and perused the

material available on record.

6.
It is not in dispute that the petitioner's daughter was married to respondent

No. 4. It is equally undisputed that she died by committing suicide within

one year of the marriage. Pursuant thereto, an FIR came to be registered

against respondent No. 4 and his family members. The petitioner also

instituted a complaint under Section 406 of the Indian Penal Code read

with Section 6 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, which is pending

adjudication. The proceedings pending before the learned Chief Judicial

Magistrate, Samba, came to be stayed by this Hon'ble Court at the

instance of respondent No.4. The petitioner had also approached the

competent Army Authorities by way of complaints seeking initiation of

appropriate disciplinary proceedings against respondent No. 4 on the

[[page 4]]

allegation that he had secured employment in the Army by

misrepresenting and fabricating his date of birth.  Despite being fully

aware of the alleged fabrication of the date of birth by respondent No. 4,

the petitioner solemnized the marriage of his daughter with respondent

No. 4. This fact is borne out from the Marriage Certificate, which reflects

respondent No.4 to be approximately seven years younger than the

petitioner's daughter. The said circumstance assumes significance while

appreciating the conduct of the petitioner and the allegations now sought

to be raised against respondent No. 4.

7.
In view of the foregoing, no case is made out for interference by this

Court in the exercise of its extraordinary writ jurisdiction under Article

226 of the Constitution of India. The present petition, having been found

to be bereft of merit and motivated by extraneous considerations, deserves

no indulgence. Accordingly, the Writ Petition stands dismissed. There

shall be no order as to costs. Pending applications, if any, shall also stand

disposed of.
